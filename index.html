    How it works

    1. Set your destination

      Search a stop, tap the map, or paste a Google Maps link.

    2. See the real road distance

      We fetch the actual road route and follow your bus along it live.

    3. Get woken up on time

      A loud alarm, vibration and notification before you reach.

    Why it's accurate

    • Road distance, not a straight line. Your GPS is matched onto the actual road route (OpenStreetMap + OSRM), so the countdown is the distance the bus really drives — even through U-turns and loops.
    • ETA from your bus's real pace. Measured over the last few minutes, including signals and stops, and it reacts within about two minutes when the bus speeds up.
    • Re-routes automatically. If the driver takes another road, we notice and recalculate.
    • GPS-loss safety net. In tunnels or dead zones we keep counting down from your last speed and lean early, never late.

    Tested on 600+ simulated rides on the real Hyderabad → Warangal road: with normal GPS the alarm rings within a few dozen metres of the point you choose.

    Questions

    Does it work with the screen off?

    Phones pause websites when the screen is locked, so during a trip Hibernate Express keeps your screen awake. Night mode turns it almost black to save battery. Don't lock the phone or leave the app for long.

    Does it need internet?

    Only to search places and fetch the road route when you start (and to re-route if the bus changes roads). Tracking and the alarm keep working offline once the trip has started.

    Is my location shared with anyone?

    No account, no tracking. Your position stays on your phone; only the start and end points are sent to the open-source routing server to calculate the road route.

    Can I use it on trains?

    Yes — switch to Train. Trains don't follow roads, so we use the straight-line distance to your station, which makes the alarm ring a little early.

    iPhone tips

    Tap Share → Add to Home Screen and open it from there to also get notifications. The alarm sound plays even when the silent switch is on; keep the media volume up.
